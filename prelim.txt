The Literacy Luminaries — Website Vision
Business Name: The Literacy Luminaries
Tagline: Lighting the Way to Literacy
Domain: literacyluminaries.com
Who We Are
The Literacy Luminaries is a collaboration of three experienced literacy professionals with extensive backgrounds in literacy instruction, dyslexia, special education, intervention, and supporting students with diverse learning needs.
We want to be viewed as literacy experts and academic therapists—not tutors.
Our work is grounded in explicit, systematic, evidence-based instruction while remaining highly individualized. We believe instruction should follow the learner rather than forcing every learner through the same pathway.
What We Plan to Offer
Initially, the website should allow room for three areas of the business:
Academic Therapy
Specialized, individualized literacy intervention for students who need more than traditional tutoring. This may include reading, dyslexia, spelling, handwriting, written language, and related literacy needs. We will not offer math therapy.
Literacy Resources & Curriculum
Eventually, we want to create and sell our own original literacy resources and instructional materials. One major project will be a completely redesigned and independently created dysgraphia/writing intervention framework inspired by our professional experience.
Professional Learning
Eventually, we may provide training, consultation, workshops, courses, and resources for educators and parents, and possibly schools/districts.
The website should be designed so these areas can be added or expanded over time. We don't need everything operational at launch.
Brand Personality
We want the site to feel:
Professional • Sophisticated • Warm • Knowledgeable • Approachable • Credible • Modern
We do not want it to look like a typical children's tutoring website.
Avoid overly juvenile graphics, primary colors, cartoon school supplies, ABC blocks, crayons, etc. Although we work with children, parents and educators are the customers, so the site should communicate expertise and trust.
There can still be personality and warmth—we don't want it to feel clinical or corporate.
Color Palette
Our general palette is:
Burgundy • Pink • Black • Gray • White
Exact color codes will come later.
Burgundy should probably serve as the dominant/signature color, with pink used intentionally as an accent. Black and gray provide sophistication and structure, and white should give the website plenty of breathing room.
Visual Identity
Because Luminaries relates to light, we're interested in subtle imagery involving:
light • illumination • rays • pathways • growth • books/language
We'd love the visual identity to subtly represent three because there are three founding Literacy Luminaries—perhaps three rays, three abstract elements, or another understated design feature.
We don't want the light concept to become literal or cheesy. No giant cartoon lightbulbs. 😂
Website Structure
For the first version, we're imagining something relatively simple:
Home | About Us | Academic Therapy | Resources | Professional Learning | Contact
Eventually, we may need:
Shop | Courses/Training | Our Writing Program | Parent Resources | Educator Resources
The architecture should make it easy to add those later without rebuilding the entire site.
Homepage Message
Someone landing on our website should understand within a few seconds:
The Literacy Luminaries
Lighting the Way to Literacy
Specialized literacy support grounded in expertise, evidence, and the individual needs of every learner.
Then introduce the three major pathways:
Academic Therapy
Individualized literacy intervention.
Professional Learning
Practical learning for educators grounded in literacy research and classroom experience.
Literacy Resources
Thoughtfully designed tools and resources that translate evidence into instruction.
Something Very Important to Us
We don't want messaging that suggests:
“Your child just needs more practice.”
Our philosophy is much closer to:
Identify the need → provide explicit instruction → monitor response → adjust instruction → build independence and confidence.
The website should communicate that we understand why students struggle and know how to design instruction in response.
Academic Therapy vs. Tutoring
This distinction should eventually have its own section because it is central to our positioning.
Traditional tutoring often helps a student with current classroom content or assignments.
Our academic therapy is intended to address underlying literacy skills through individualized, explicit, systematic intervention.
We don't want to disparage tutors—we simply want families to understand that we're providing a different type of service.
Meet the Luminaries
The three founders should each have a professional photo, short biography, credentials/expertise, and something personal enough that families get a sense of who we are.
We want visitors to think:
“These women know literacy.”
But also:
“I would trust them with my child.”
Build With the Future in Mind
We're intentionally starting this as a small retirement/side business, so we don't want to overbuild the first website.
Version 1 should be beautiful, professional, simple and easy to maintain.
But if the business grows, we'd like the site to be capable of eventually supporting:
online scheduling/intake • payments • digital resource sales • curriculum sales • educator courses • professional development registration • email/newsletter sign-ups
We don't necessarily need those features at launch—we just don't want to design ourselves into a corner.
One sentence that captures the whole vision
The Literacy Luminaries is a literacy-focused academic therapy and educational resource practice created by three experienced literacy professionals who believe expert, individualized instruction can illuminate the path toward confident, independent readers and writers.
