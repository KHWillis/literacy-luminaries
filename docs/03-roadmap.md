# Roadmap / Phasing

## Phase 0 — Decisions & setup (before building)
- Finalize (or accept as "likely final") business name/domain
- Register domain + set up business email
- Pick tech stack (see `01-tech-stack-options.md`)
- Gather content: founder bios/photos, service descriptions, logo/brand assets
- Rough color codes + font pairing chosen (burgundy/pink/black/gray/white)

## Phase 1 — V1 Launch site
Scope: `Home | About Us | Academic Therapy | Resources | Professional Learning | Contact`
- Home: hero w/ tagline, three-pathway intro, brief credibility signals
- About Us / Meet the Luminaries: 3 founder bios + photos + credentials
- Academic Therapy: what it is, who it's for, Academic Therapy vs. Tutoring distinction
- Resources: placeholder/coming soon framing — no e-commerce yet
- Professional Learning: placeholder/coming soon framing
- Contact: simple form or mailto, maybe address/service-area note
- Newsletter signup (optional, low-lift addition to start building an audience early)
- Basic SEO (titles/meta, sitemap), analytics, accessibility pass

## Phase 2 — Growth features (as the business grows)
Add incrementally, no rebuild required if Phase 1 architecture is chosen well:
- Online scheduling/intake (e.g., embed Calendly/Acuity, or a form-based intake flow)
- Email newsletter automation (if not already in place)
- Blog / articles section (SEO + authority-building)

## Phase 3 — Monetization features
- Digital resource sales (e.g., Stripe Payment Links, Gumroad, or a proper digital
  storefront once there's a real catalog)
- Curriculum sales (the dysgraphia/writing framework) — likely its own product page(s)
- Educator courses/training — may need a course platform (e.g., Teachable/Thinkific)
  linked from the main site rather than built in-house
- Professional development registration/payment

## Notes on sequencing
- Nothing in Phase 1 should block Phases 2–3. That mainly means: choose a CMS/content
  model where "Resources" and "Professional Learning" are already modeled as expandable
  sections (even if they just say "coming soon" at launch), not one-off static pages
  that need to be rebuilt later.
- Most Phase 2/3 features are additions of third-party embeds/integrations rather than
  custom development — keeps maintenance low, consistent with the "low maintenance"
  goal.
